# P7 Keeper's Dilemmas: build plan (2026-11-02)

Tests **H7**: *when the people ask the Keeper to choose between their own proposals, the history feels like the
player's doing (authorship), without feeling like a quiz or a chore.*
Why: the game lacks choices whose consequences the player can see and remembers (user, 2026-11-02). Design and options:
[research 14 §2](../../research/14-dilemmas-and-living-land.md). Research behind the rules: §2.9 there.
Issues: milestone **P7 Keeper's dilemmas**, label `proto:P7`: #220 sim core, #221 callbacks, #222 the 8 dilemmas, #223 card, #224 choice scene, #225 debug/telemetry/survey, #226 test and results. Results go here after testing.

## The rules P7 builds (from research)
1. **Two options, both good.** Different histories, never a better economy. No numbers shown. (Reigns: binary fits a
   phone; Telltale critique: a "right answer" turns a choice into a test.)
2. **The player can see what each option means.** A one-line consequence hint per option in the people's voice
   ("Ada will run it her way, and Bo won't like it"). (*Awareness*: a choice whose result can't be predicted feels
   arbitrary.)
3. **The answer changes how things play, not just a line.** The chosen person really holds the role, or the lean
   really moves what gets built, or strangers really arrive. (*Gameplay consequences*.)
4. **It comes back.** Every option has at least one **callback** story, 4–15 game years later in P7 (5–30 in the
   full game), credited: *"Because the Keeper chose Ada…"*. (*Reminders*; King of Dragon Pass's delayed scenes.)
5. **It sticks.** No undo once answered. (*Permanence*.)
6. **Silence is safe.** Unanswered for 24 game months, the people decide (the role-holder's trait, else a seeded
   pick), and the chronicle says so neutrally. No timer shown, no blame. (Pillar 4; wishes already work this way.)
7. **Storylet shape** (Failbetter / Emily Short): a dilemma is *conditions → content → effects*; a callback is a
   storylet whose condition is "this option was chosen N years ago, and the people in it are still around".

## The one variable
- **A:** the slice as it is (wishes only).
- **B:** the slice + dilemmas (`SimConfig.dilemmas = true`), answered **in the box** (touch one of two people or
  places) with **two buttons on the card** as a fallback. Which path the player uses is logged, not varied.

## Scope
- **In:** 8 dilemmas (4 camp, 4 village) of the kinds *who*, *which way*, *tradition*, *strangers*; each option with
  effects and 1–2 callbacks; the card; the choice scene in the box; the lapse; telemetry; the survey questions;
  debug cheats.
- **Out (after P7, if it passes):** the Keeper's character leanings (research 14 §2.6), land dilemmas (P8), the book
  page and the time-lapse hand pins, town-era dilemmas.

## Pacing
- At most **one open**, and it **shares the wish slot** (a wish *or* a dilemma, never both); a gap of 8 game months
  after each, as for wishes.
- None during the first run (until `FirstRun` is complete).
- About one per **6–10 game years** in normal play [est]. In the lab (×60), the first comes at year 2 and then about
  every 3 years, so a 10-minute lab session sees 2–3.
- A dilemma waits on the card stack like any card; a dilemma raised while away is shown on return (after the era card,
  before the wish slot).

## Measures
- **Telemetry:** `dilemma_raised` (id, month), `dilemma_shown`, `dilemma_answered` (id, option, path `box|card`,
  seconds from shown, years open), `dilemma_lapsed` (id, default option), `callback_shown` (id, option, years since
  the answer), `callback_showme`.
- **Survey:** Q4 = "What happened in the box felt like my doing" (1–5). Extra Q6 (free text): "Did any question
  feel like it had a wrong answer, or like homework?" Day-2 recall prompt (`RecallPrompt`): "Name one choice you made
  for the people, and what came of it."
- **Where the words live (#225):** `Runtime/Telemetry/DilemmaSurvey.cs` (Q4, Q6, the recall question, variant letters A = off, B = on);
  `RecallPrompt.ShouldAsk(state, ctx, DilemmaFirstDay, DilemmaLastDay)` asks on day 2 (window 2-4); the default stays day 3.
  The variant is in every `session_start` (`variant`) and in a `variant` event when it changes mid-session. Every dilemma event
  carries `box` and the sim `id`. `TelemetryAnalyzer` reports per variant: raised/shown/answered/lapsed, answered share
  (answered / (answered + lapsed), shown dilemmas only), box-vs-card split, median and range of seconds to answer, callbacks shown and Show me.

## Pass (go to the full design) when
- B beats A on Q4 by **≥ 1 point** on average, and Q1 (chore … relaxing) is **not lower** in B.
- **≥ 70 %** of shown dilemmas are answered (not left to lapse) in the lab.
- **≥ 2/3** of diary testers recall a choice *and* its result on day 2.
- **Nobody** reports a "wrong answer" feeling or homework in Q6.
- Informative, not pass/fail: the box-vs-card path split; the seconds to answer; callbacks opened with Show me.

## Protocol
- **Lab** (~10 min a variant, ×60, fresh box each): A then B for half the testers, B then A for the rest.
- **Diary** (3 days, real time, B only): measures recall and fatigue over normal check-ins.
- The **tester agent** plays B first (fresh box, both answer paths, a lapse, a callback) before any human sees it.

## Dilemmas (#222, for tone review)

The eight dilemmas, as written in `Sim/DilemmaDefs.cs`: four camp, four village, two of each kind. `{a}`/`{b}` are the two
people; `{place}` is the settlement's name ("the camp" / "the village" before it has one). Every callback is told after
*"Because the Keeper chose X, "* (or *"Because they chose X, "* when the people decided after a lapse), 4–15 game years
later; "…" below marks that prefix. A callback with a fallback tells the fallback when its condition fails; one without is
dropped quietly.

How the words are picked:
- **The asker.** The era's leader (chief, headman) asks, if they aren't one of the two; in a *who* dilemma the eldest
  other adult asks instead (a leader never asks who replaces them). Some traits have their own wording; any other trait
  gets the plain question.
- **Inside the box.** The people live in the box: nobody comes from or goes back to anywhere outside it. Strangers come
  from the far woods or across the pond (the content test bans "over the hill", "beyond", "outside" and the like).
- **Who hints.** In a *who* dilemma each option's hint comes from that person's trait (14 lines per dilemma), so the
  player can see what kind of leader they're picking. The plain hint is the fallback.
- **A lapse.** For a *who*, the old leader's ties pick (#220). For the others, the asker's trait leans to the option it
  favours (listed below), else a seeded pick.
- **Both good.** A lean is food *or* wood for 36 months (as a shaping's), never both, never more. Strangers (more people)
  are weighed against a lean in the other option. Traditions only store a custom and come back as stories.

### 1. `camp_fire`: the chief is getting old (Who, camp) — revised after review
- **When:** only while the chief is an elder (45+, `SimConfig.elderAge`). The eldest other adult asks, never the chief
  (with no other adult, the plain question is shown).
- **Effect:** the chosen person becomes chief now (the old chief steps down); the other gets a grudge or grace by trait.
- **Question:** "The chief is getting old. Who leads next: {a} or {b}?"
- **Asked by an elder:** gossip "Chief's slowing down. Everyone says {a} or {b}. Which?" · worrier "The chief is old. Who
  will lead us next: {a} or {b}?" · kind "Let the chief rest at last. Who leads next: {a} or {b}?" · grump "Chief's past
  it. Don't tell them I said so. {a} or {b}?" · joker "The chief fell asleep mid-speech again. {a} or {b}?" · stubborn
  "The chief won't say it, so I will. Next: {a} or {b}?"
- **Options:** "{a}" / "{b}". Plain hint: "{a} will lead {place} their own way, and {b} will watch."
- **Hint by the person's trait** ({p} = that person): stubborn "{p} will lead one way, and never turn back." · dreamer
  "…will lead with big ideas, and some of them will work." · kind "…will lead gently, and nobody will go hungry." · loud
  "…will lead from the front, and everyone will hear it." · clever "…will lead with a plan for everything. Twice." · lazy
  "…will lead calmly. Very calmly. Mostly sitting down." · brave "…will lead the hunts farther out, and come back
  first." · gossip "…will lead by knowing everything about everyone." · gardener "…will lead {place} toward planting and
  tending." · grump "…will lead fairly, and complain the whole way." · singer "…will lead with a song for every job." ·
  tinkerer "…will lead by fixing things nobody knew were broken." · worrier "…will lead carefully, with a plan for every
  storm." · joker "…will lead with a laugh, and {place} will follow."
- **Keeper line:** "The old chief stepped down, and the Keeper chose {a} to lead."
- **Callbacks** (chosen = {a}; mirrored for {b}):
  - ({a} alive) "…{place} still does things {a}'s way, down to who sits where at supper." Fallback: "…{place} still does
    things the way {a} did, down to who sits where at supper."
  - (both alive, 5+ years) "…{b} never got to lead, but has given {a} advice every day since. Some of it was good." No
    fallback.

### 2. `camp_winter`: baskets or woodpile (Which way, camp; needs the woodpile)
- **Question:** "Before the snow: fill the baskets, or the woodpile?"
- **Asked by the chief:** worrier "Winter's coming. It always is. Baskets or woodpile?" · lazy "We can only do one before
  winter. Baskets or wood?" · gardener "Bushes heavy, nights cold. Baskets or woodpile?" · grump "Cold soon. Hungry soon.
  Pick one: baskets or wood?" · tinkerer "I have a basket idea and a log idea. Which first?"
- **"the baskets"**: "Full bellies all winter, and a thinner woodpile." Effect: food lean; custom `winter:baskets`;
  points at the gathering grounds. A lapse leans here for kind, gardener, lazy, dreamer, singer.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper chose the baskets, and everyone went berrying."
  - "…{place} still goes berrying every autumn, all together, and nobody comes home with clean fingers."
  - (6+ years) "…the children of {place} know every berry bush by name. Some of the names are rude."
- **"the woodpile"**: "Warm huts and new building, and a plainer winter table." Effect: wood lean; custom
  `winter:wood`; points at the woodpile. Lapse: tinkerer, brave, stubborn, clever, worrier.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper chose the woodpile. The axes rang till the first snow."
  - "…{place}'s woodpile grew so tall it got a little roof of its own."
  - (6+ years) "…the roofs of {place} have never once leaked. People knock on them to show visitors."

### 3. `camp_remember`: the stone or the tree (Tradition, camp)
- **Question:** "Where do we remember the ones we lose: stone or tree?"
- **Asked by the chief:** kind "Nobody should be forgotten. The stone, or the tree?" · dreamer "I dreamt of the ones
  before us. Stone or tree?" · singer "Where do I sing for the ones we lose: stone or tree?" · worrier "What if we forget
  someone? Stone or tree, to remember?" · stubborn "Stone. Obviously. But some say the tree. Well?"
- **"the stone"**: "Names on the big stone by the pond, there for good." Effect: custom `remember:stone`. Lapse:
  stubborn, clever, grump, brave, tinkerer.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper chose the stone. The first name went on it that spring."
  - "…the big stone by the pond is covered in names now, and the children polish it with their sleeves."
  - (7+ years) "…anyone in {place} can tell you who every name on the stone was. Some of the stories are even true."
- **"the tree"**: "A charm on the old tree for each of them, growing with it." Effect: custom `remember:tree`. Lapse:
  kind, gardener, dreamer, singer, worrier.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper chose the old tree. The first charm went up that spring."
  - "…the old tree is hung with little charms, and they click together in the wind like someone laughing."
  - (7+ years) "…nobody in {place} will cut a branch from the old tree. Not even the low one that hits everyone on
    the head."

### 4. `camp_travellers`: lost travellers from the far woods (Strangers, camp)
- **Question:** "Lost travellers at the edge of the woods. Can they stay?"
- **Asked by the chief:** kind "Two lost travellers, cold and hungry. Can they stay?" · worrier "Strangers! Two of them.
  Probably fine? Can they stay?" · gossip "Strangers in the woods! I saw them first. Can they stay?" · brave "I found two
  travellers in the woods. Can they stay?" · grump "Two more mouths at the edge of the woods. Can they stay?"
- **"a place by the fire"**: "Two new faces round the fire, and new ways of doing things." Effect: a couple joins (two
  new notables, one family); custom `travellers:stayed`. Lapse: kind, singer, gossip, dreamer, joker, loud.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper let the travellers stay. They slept by the fire the first night and never left."
  - "…the travellers who stayed still cook their own strange stew. Half of {place} asks for it now."
  - (6+ years) "…the travellers' way of tying a knot caught on. Everything in {place} is tied twice now."
- **"full packs"**: "They'll camp in the far woods, and leave us their trick with an axe." (revised: they stay inside the box) Effect: wood lean; custom
  `travellers:sent`. Lapse: grump, worrier, stubborn, clever, tinkerer, lazy.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper sent the travellers on with full packs. They showed everyone a thing or two with an axe
    first."
  - "…every axe in {place} still has the travellers' little notch in the handle. Nobody knows why it helps. It helps."
  - (6+ years, revised) "…the travellers still live wild in the far woods. Some evenings you can see their fire, and all
    of {place} waves at it."

### 5. `village_headman`: the vote is coming (Who, village) — revised after review
- **When:** only in the 24 months before the headman's term ends (an election is due). The eldest other adult asks,
  never the headman.
- **Effect:** the chosen person becomes headman now (the headman hands over early); the other gets a grudge or grace by
  trait.
- **Question:** "A vote soon, and {place} is split. Headman: {a} or {b}?"
- **Asked by an elder:** gossip "Everyone's whispering about the vote. {a} or {b}?" · grump "Another vote, another row.
  Headman: {a} or {b}?" · kind "Let's not fall out over the vote. {a} or {b}?" · lazy "Counting votes is hard. Just
  pick: {a} or {b}?" · worrier "What if the vote comes out even? {a} or {b}?"
- **Options:** "{a}" / "{b}". Plain hint: "{a} will run {place} their way, and {b} will watch."
- **Hint by the person's trait:** stubborn "{p} will run {place} one way only. A straight way." · dreamer "…will have big
  plans for {place}, and some will happen." · kind "…will see that nobody goes without, and talk to everyone." · loud
  "…will hold meetings you can hear from the pond." · clever "…will settle every quarrel with a clever new rule." · lazy
  "…will keep the meetings short and the benches comfy." · brave "…will stand up for {place} first and ask after." ·
  gossip "…will know what everyone wants before they say it." · gardener "…will put flowers by every bench and tidy the
  fields." · grump "…will grumble at everyone fairly, and get things done." · singer "…will open every meeting with a
  song. A long one." · tinkerer "…will mend everything in {place}, starting with the well." · worrier "…will check on
  every hearth, twice, and keep {place} careful." · joker "…will make the meetings fun, and nobody will skip them."
- **Keeper line:** "The vote was split, and the Keeper chose {a} as headman."
- **Callbacks** (chosen = {a}; mirrored for {b}):
  - ({a} alive) "…the meetings by the well still go {a}'s way, and they still start late." Fallback: "…{place} still
    holds its meetings the way {a} did, and they still start late."
  - ({b} alive, 5+ years) "…{b} sits at the back of every meeting with folded arms, and has the best ideas there." No
    fallback.

### 6. `village_spring`: the miller or the roofers (Which way, village; needs the mill) — revised after review
- **Question:** "Miller or roofers: who gets the young ones this year?"
- **Asked by the headman:** gardener "The miller and the roofers both want the young ones. Who?" · tinkerer "Miller or
  roofers? I made a ladder for either. Who?" · worrier "Roofs leaking, grain ripe. Who gets the young ones?" · loud
  "MILLER OR ROOFERS? Sorry. Who gets the young ones?" · lazy "Everyone wants the young ones but me. Miller or roofers?"
  · grump "The miller's shouting and the roofers are shouting. Who?"
- **"the miller"**: "Full sacks and fresh bread, and the roofs drip a while longer." Effect: food lean; custom
  `spring:miller`; points at the mill. Lapse: gardener, kind, singer, lazy, worrier.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper sent the young ones to the miller. The mill sang all summer."
  - (the mill stands) "…the miller still calls them "my young ones", though they're all grown and one has a beard."
    Fallback: "…{place} still bakes the best bread anyone remembers, and says so to everyone."
  - (5+ years) "…{place} had so much flour one autumn that the children had a flour fight. There was no winner."
- **"the roofers"**: "Dry roofs and new rafters, and the bread a little thin." Effect: wood lean; custom
  `spring:roofers`; points at the huts. Lapse: tinkerer, clever, stubborn, brave, loud.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper sent the young ones up the ladders. Sawdust got into everything, including the soup."
  - "…every roof the young ones mended that year has a little carved bird on the ridge. Now every new roof gets one."
  - (5+ years) "…the young roofers of that year still climb up to watch the sunset. Nobody can get them down for
    supper."

### 7. `village_harvest`: feast or offering (Tradition, village)
- **Question:** "How do we mark the harvest: a feast or an offering?"
- **Asked by the headman:** singer "The harvest needs a song. A feast song, or an offering one?" · joker "Harvest's in!
  Do we eat it, or give it to the Keeper?" · kind "Share the harvest round our tables, or with the Keeper?" · dreamer
  "Maybe the Keeper is hungry too. Feast or offering?" · grump "Either way I carry the tables. Feast or offering?"
- **"a feast"**: "Long tables with everyone at them, and the Keeper watching." Effect: custom `harvest:feast`. Lapse:
  loud, joker, singer, gossip, lazy.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper chose a feast. The tables ran the length of {place}."
  - "…the harvest feast at {place} lasts three days now. Nobody remembers agreeing to the third."
  - (the tavern stands, 5+ years) "…the tavern keeps one long table just for the harvest feast, and nobody may eat at it
    the rest of the year." No fallback.
- **"an offering"**: "The best basket left on the hill for the Keeper, and quiet thanks." Effect: custom
  `harvest:offering`. Lapse: kind, dreamer, worrier, gardener, stubborn.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper chose an offering. The best basket went up the hill, very carefully."
  - "…the best basket goes up the hill every autumn, and every spring it's empty. The birds aren't saying."
  - (5+ years) "…the children of {place} leave a crumb on the sill for the Keeper after every meal. Just in case."

### 8. `village_hill_family`: the family across the pond (Strangers, village) — revised after review
(The id is kept so code and tests still find it; nothing is "over the hill" any more.)
- **Question:** "The family across the pond asks for a home. Here, or there?"
- **Asked by the headman:** kind "The family across the pond wants a home. Where?" · stubborn "A family wants a home.
  Here, or on their own shore?" · joker "A family across the pond. Here, or near enough to wave?" · loud "A FAMILY!
  Across the pond! Where do they live?" · gossip "I've met the pond family already. Here, or there?"
- **"a home here"**: "New neighbours at the next hearth, and new names in {place}." Effect: a family of three joins;
  custom `pond:joined`. Lapse: kind, gossip, singer, loud, joker.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper gave the pond family a home here. They moved in with a goat."
  - "…the pond family still sing their shore songs at supper. Half of {place} knows the words now."
  - (5+ years) "…the pond family's goat has eaten its way through every garden in {place}. It is much loved anyway."
- **"a home across the pond"**: "They'll keep their own shore, and trade us their catch." Effect: food lean; custom
  `pond:neighbours`. Lapse: grump, worrier, stubborn, gardener, lazy.
  - Keeper line: "The Keeper left the pond family on their shore. They waved, and sent fish."
  - "…the family across the pond still rows over every spring with fish. Far too many fish."
  - (6+ years) "…there's a path worn round the pond now, from {place} to the neighbours and back. Nobody built it.
    Everyone walked it."

### Open points for the review
- The traditions (`camp_remember`, `village_harvest`) only store a custom: nothing in the world or the book reads it
  yet, so in P7 their callbacks are the only visible result.
- Leans are only food or wood (`SimCore.Lean`), so both *which way* dilemmas are food against wood with different
  flavour. A placement lean (research 14 §2.4) would need a new sim layer.
- `camp_remember` (about the dead) is the one sombre question. It's gentle, but say if it's too heavy for the slice.
- In seeds 1–12, the village dilemmas come 10–17 game years in (after the village era starts); `village_spring` needs
  the mill, so it holds in about 7 of 12 seeds within 40 years. After the review, `camp_fire` waits for an elder chief
  (19 of 20 seeds within 40 years; in real play only when the chief is 45+ before the village) and `village_headman`
  for the 24 months before an election (15 of 20 seeds).
- The strangers' chronicle line now says the family "came in from the far woods" (it said "came from beyond").
