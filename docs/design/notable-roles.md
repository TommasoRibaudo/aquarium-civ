## 1. Everyday jobs

Every adult notable (16+) has one job, shown on their card ("Ivo Lund, hunter"). Children play; past ~95 [est] a notable
retires to **elder** (tells stories at the fire, minds the children). A job needs its building or land feature to exist.
At an era change, a notable whose job has gone takes the nearest one (hunter → shepherd, gatherer → farmer).
Jobs are view and story only: the crowd's work still drives the aggregate economy.

| Era | Job | Enabled by | Seen doing in the box |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camp | **Gatherer** | Always (berry bushes; more after the Keeper's rain) | Picks at bushes, carries a basket back to the fire |
| Camp | **Hunter** | Forest edge; a herd (research 10 tool 18) | Walks out with a spear, crouches, comes back with a hide for the drying rack (the kill stays off-screen) |
| Camp | **Fisher** | The pond | Stands on the bank or wades, carries fish on a stick |

## 2. Important roles per era

A role has a **seat**: the building it works from. A role opens when its seat exists, so roles appear one by one as the
settlement grows. Roles carry over between eras where it makes sense: **the chief becomes the headman, the headman
becomes the mayor** (same person, a chronicle line).

| Era | Role | Seat | What they do in the box |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camp (1) | **Chief** | The camp fire (`Camp`) | Speaks at the fire in the evening; leads the hunt when the Keeper sends a herd; settles quarrels (stands between two arguing rivals) |
| Village (3) | **Headman** | The well and the green (`Well`) | Morning round of the fields; speaks on the green; opens festivals; greets newcomers |
| Village | **Miller** | `Windmill` | Opens the sails, carries sacks, takes grain from the farmers; flour dust |
| Village | **Priest** | `Church` (a small shrine in the village) | Rings the bell, blesses the harvest and weddings, sits with the dying, climbs the Keeper's hill |
| Town (7) | **Mayor** | `Market` square (a town hall later) | Speaks from the market steps; leads the yearly procession; signs things |
| Town | **Priest** | `Church` | As in the village |
| Town | **Miller** | `Windmill` (or a watermill on the stream) | As in the village |
| Town | **Guildmaster** | A guild hall [new]; the `Tavern` until it exists | Walks the workshops inspecting work; chairs the guild meeting; hangs the guild banner |
| Town | **Master builder** | The quarry or timber yard (`Lumbermill` until a quarry exists) | Stands at building sites pointing; measures with a rod; the scaffolding is theirs |
| Town | **Merchant** | `Market` | Haggles at the big stall, loads a cart, counts coins |
| Town | **Captain of the watch** | A watch house by the gate [new]; the `Market` until it exists | Lights lanterns at dusk, does the night round, finds lost goats and children |

A role-holder keeps their job ("Pell Mill, farmer and miller"): their day is split, the role first, then the job.

Settings may rename a role later (desert oasis: the headman is the **water-judge**, research 09's Water-Judge's Cup).
Not in the slice.

## 3. How a role is won

Who can stand: a living adult notable (25+ [est]) with no other role. Each candidate gets a **standing** score:
aspiration (their trait wants this role, §4) + age and experience + friends and family among the notables − rivals
+ a die from `storyRng` [est weights]. A notable who doesn't aspire can still win, which makes good stories (§5 line 8).

| Role | How it's won | Term | The drama it makes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chief | **Contest**: the founder is the first chief. When the seat is empty, aspirants are judged at the fire (a hunt, a speech); the highest standing wins. Every ~15 years [est] a rival may **challenge** | Until death, stepping down (~95 [est]) or a lost challenge | Challenges between rivals |
| Headman | **Election** by the notables (each votes by friendship, family, rivalry, trait) | 12 years [est], re-electable | Close votes, sore losers |
| Miller | **Succession**: the first holder is whoever the mill was built for; then their apprentice or eldest child | For life | Family lines; a passed-over child |
| Priest | **Succession by naming**: the priest names a successor before they die (often their apprentice); the first priest is chosen by the founding of the shrine | For life | A surprise choice |
| Mayor | **Election** (as headman) | 12 years [est] | Campaigns, recounts |
| Guildmaster | **Election by the guild**: only notables with a craft job vote and stand | 12 years [est] | Craft against craft |
| Master builder | **Contest of work**: when the seat is empty, the craft notables each build something; the best wins | For life | Rival chimneys |
| Merchant | **Appointment** by the mayor | Until the next mayor | Favours for friends: an appointed friend of the mayor angers the mayor's rivals |
| Captain of the watch | **Appointment** by the mayor | Until the next mayor | As above |

**Rivals compete.** If two rivals stand for the same role, the contest card ranks higher (+0.1 salience [est]), and a
loser with no rival may take the winner as one. **Death in office** frees the seat at once; the contest comes the next
spring (a short empty seat is a story).

## 4. Traits: what they want and how they colour a term

The 14 traits are the ones in `NameData.Traits`. "Wants" sets the aspiration bonus in §3. "In office" is what changes
while they hold *any* role, through **story weights, chronicle lines and the activity planner only**. It never touches
`econRng`, food, wood, mood or output. Extra festivals or feasts a role-holder throws are view-and-story gatherings with
no mood bonus; the economy's harvest festival is unchanged.

| Trait | Wants | In office (story + behaviour) |
|---|---|---|
| the brave | Chief, captain of the watch | Expeditions and hunts; "lost in the hills" stories come home with more; kids play chase near the seat |
| the stubborn | Chief, headman, mayor, master builder | Long meetings; refuses changes ("the path now goes around them" stories); hardly ever steps down |
| the kind | Headman, mayor, priest | Shared meals at the seat; reconciliations ×2 [est], new feuds ½; visits the old and sick |
| the loud | Chief, captain of the watch, merchant | Announcements: the crowd gathers to listen more often; "loud" stories ×2 |
| the clever | Guildmaster, master builder, miller | Inventions ×2 [est]; a strange device appears by the seat |
| the tinkerer | Miller, master builder | As clever; the mill or scaffolding sprouts extra bits |
| the dreamer | Priest | Grand plans that half happen; strange lights and maps; pilgrimages to the Keeper's hill |
| the singer | Priest, chief | Singing at the fire or green most evenings; more feast gatherings (the user's "cheerful chief") |
| the joker | Mayor, merchant | Pranks ×2; feast days with games; a role-holder's speech ends in laughter |
| the gossip | Headman, merchant | Friendships *and* rivalries form faster (both ×1.5 [est]); everyone knows everything |
| the grump | Captain of the watch, guildmaster | A tidy, quiet place (the user's "stern headman"): people go home earlier at night, sweepers busier, fewer feasts, more complaint stories |
| the worrier | Captain of the watch, priest | Fences, extra lanterns, people home early; storm stories end with everyone safe in the seat building |
| the gardener | Miller, priest | Flowers round the seat; the green gets planted; harvest blessings |
| the lazy | Nothing | Short work days in the view (naps on the green, long lunches); output unchanged. Comic if elected anyway |

The user's "quarrelsome guildmaster" maps to **the stubborn** or **the grump** with a rival: their term adds feuds
(rivalries ×1.5 [est]) and meeting rows.
