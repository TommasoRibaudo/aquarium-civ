## 1. The five relics

Shared rules (research 09 §1.3, §2): every relic comes from the sim's own story, never from mess, never bought, rolled
or timed; one per idea per box; nothing missable forever (each has a fallback); every relic is born in a chronicle
card and gets a label in the people's voice. Cards: `{0}` = the notable, `{1}` = a second notable, `{2}` = the place.
Card bodies are the narrator (may say "glass"); labels, hints and quotes are the people (era words).

### 1.1 The Sky Crumb (camp, stage 1)
- **Trigger:** the box's **second shaping** of any kind (first session: usually the grove). Box 1 only in the slice.
- **The moment:** a biscuit crumb the size of a hut drops in from the top of the box with a soft thud and a puff of
  flour, next to the shaping. Everyone freezes and steps back. The kids creep up first, the bravest notable pokes it,
  the chief tastes it, then a cheer, and people carry chunks to the fire. It shrinks over the next game months as it
  is eaten; the last piece stays on a flat stone by the fire (the relic).
- **Card** (salience 0.9): "A piece of bread the size of a hut fell from the sky the year the Keeper planted the grove.
  {0} tasted it first. The camp ate well all winter."
- **Label:** "Sky Bread. Fell when the Bright One made the trees. Delicious." (Fix: chronicle §5.3 says "made rain";
  the crumb now comes with the second shaping.)
- **Does:** H. A yearly "Sky Bread Day" feast in that box (a view gathering, no mood effect). Carried over: box 2's
  founders unpack it at their first fire and the chief tells the story (era-moment §8 founders line).
- **Belief:** opens stage 1's later half: things come *from* the sky, so now they gather at the edge to look up.
- **Book / shelf:** the first relic page (camp: a painting on stone). Shelf: a nibbled crumb on the first plinth; tap
  and a crumb falls off it.

### 1.2 The Festival Mask (camp → village, the bridge from stage 1 to 2)
- **Trigger:** the **first era festival** (the camp becomes a village, watched live).
- **The moment:** in the ring dance the leader (or a singer) wears a round bark mask of "the sky's face". They dance
  facing the glass. Nobody hides; that is the point.
- **Card** (0.75): "{2} danced for three days to welcome the new village. {0} wore a mask of the sky's face and
  nobody recognised them, though everyone did."
- **Label:** "The Sky's Face. Worn at the first village dance. Smiles on one side; the other side was still drying."
- **Does:** S. Carried over: box 2 holds a founding festival in the camp (the era festival's look and mood lift, once).
- **Belief:** marks the turn from wary to playful: from the festival on, the village lines and play start.
- **Book / shelf:** a page in the village spread. Shelf: the mask on a peg, facing you.

### 1.3 The Keeper Doll (village, stage 2)
- **Trigger:** village era and **10 credited Keeper entries** (`keeper = true`) in this box [est]. Fallback: after 15
  village years, the next credited entry makes it anyway. Maker: the priest if a dreamer or singer, else a dreamer,
  kind or singer notable, else any notable.
- **The moment:** the maker sets the doll on a post by the shrine (or the green), facing the glass. People gather;
  some bow, kids copy its pose (one hand raised, very big). The doll **holds what you do most** in this box: a cloth
  (wipes), a watering can (rain), a sapling (grove), a spade (channel), a lantern (lamp).
- **Card** (0.85): "{0} carved the Keeper and stood them by the shrine. Hands: enormous. Eye: open. Everyone agrees
  it's a good likeness."
- **Label:** "The Keeper. Hands: very large. Eye: always open." (as chronicle §5.3)
- **Does:** H. On the shelf it faces you (first-session closing option D). Carried over: box 2's first gathering at
  the glass skips the scared beat ("the old country's Keeper came with us").
- **Belief:** opens stage 2's later half: they look for you. Kids wave at the doll, then at the glass.
- **Book / shelf:** a page in the priest's hand. Shelf: the doll, turned to face the camera.

### 1.4 The Left Sandal (any era, a story you find by cleaning)
- **Trigger, part 1:** a "lost" story for a notable in the camp or village: "{0} lost a sandal crossing the stream.
  {0} has walked a little lopsided, with great dignity, ever since." The sim stores the owner and a spot.
- **Trigger, part 2:** **≥ 20 game years later** (Choice 7), the next time you **prune** overgrowth, the find pops out
  of the vines nearest the spot. It is seeded once, waits as long as needed, and needs only *some* overgrowth to prune,
  so letting weeds grow never makes more finds (research 09). Sim note: overgrowth is one number today, so the sim
  keeps the spot and the view places the find under the nearest vine patch.
- **The moment:** a sandal flips out of the cut vines. Someone picks it up, holds it up to the glass, and runs it to the
  owner, who puts it on and does a lopsided little dance. If the owner is dead, it goes to their eldest child or
  grandchild, who puts it on the remembering stone.
- **Card** (0.8): "Under the weeds by the old gathering ground, the Keeper found the left sandal of {0}, lost
  {n} years ago. {0} says it still fits. It doesn't."
- **Label:** "The Left Sandal of {0}. Lost to the stream, found by the Keeper. The right one was never seen again."
- **Does:** C, plus a returning-home card. Carried over: the owner's family carries it, and its page continues in box
  2's book.
- **Belief:** any stage: adds the "the Keeper finds lost things" lines ("Lost something? Ask the window.").
- **Book / shelf:** a page that shows the loss card and the find card side by side. Shelf: one sandal on a cushion.

### 1.5 The Lens (town, stage 3; takes the Keeper's Lamp slot, Choice 1)
- **Trigger:** town era + the **Keeper's Lamp used in the town** (it hangs over the square at dusk, research 10) + a
  scholar alive (Choice 5). Fallback: after 10 town years, any town shaping.
- **The moment:** at dusk, on a roof or the Keeper's hill, the scholar sets a brass tube on a tripod and points it at
  the lantern, then past it at the camera. A queue forms. Each person looks, leans back startled, laughs, lets the next
  one look.
- **Card** (0.9): "{0} ground a lens from the clearest glass in {2} and pointed it at the Keeper's light. They have
  not slept since."
- **Label:** "The Great Eye-Glass. Through it: a room, a lamp, and something with whiskers." (as chronicle §5.3)
- **Does:** H. From now on, town returns may get an astronomer's report ("The observatory says the room was dark for
  days. They lit a candle for you.": warm, never guilt). Carried over: a camp child in box 2 looks through it once
  and says nothing about what they saw.
- **Belief:** opens stage 3's later half: the people say "glass" (Choice 4) and the scholars study it.
- **Book / shelf:** a page in the printed town book, with a woodcut. Shelf: the lens on its tripod; tap it to look
  through it at the box.

## 3. At the glass: group behaviours per stage (for the planner)

All at the front glass (the side the camera faces). Never more than ~6 dolls at the glass, so work goes on. Keeper
reactions (life-in-the-box §8) still win over everything; this table only says how they look in each stage.

| | Stage 1 (camp) | Stage 2 (village) | Stage 3 (town) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gathering** | After a wipe or the Sky Crumb, 3–6 people stand a step back from the glass and look up; arms stay close; at most once a view day | Kids most afternoons (1–3), pressing hands to the glass; at dusk a few adults sit facing it | A scholar most mornings with a rod or slate, measuring along the glass; kids still play |
| **Hold a finger** (decided: they look up, kids gather and jump) | Kids creep in slowly, hop back, then jump under it; adults stay back | Kids race to it and follow it when it moves ("catch the finger") | Kids jump; a scholar walks over and measures it with a rod |
| **Tap (with a sound)** (decided: they look up) | Everyone looks up, a flinch, one step back, then a peek (Choice 8) | Everyone looks up; kids laugh and point at the spot | Everyone looks up; a scholar taps back the same count (Choice 9) and writes it on a slate |
| **You return** (the wave) | A wary wave, from a step back | Everyone waves; kids run to the glass and look for you | Waves; the scholars turn the lens to the camera |
| **After the relic** | Some come to look up where the bread came from | Kids wave at the doll, then at the glass | The lens slowly follows the camera as you rotate |
