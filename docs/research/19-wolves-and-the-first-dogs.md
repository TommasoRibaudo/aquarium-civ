# 19 — Wolves, and how they become the first dogs

Research date: **2026-11-07**. The user, after research 18: *"Let's add wolves. But research this thoroughly: they're
gonna hunt, bother humans, and create decisions and interaction animations. You place them, of course."* Then:
*"That should lead to dogs!"*

This builds on [research 18](18-placed-life-and-ecosystems.md) (the jar: the land gets ready, the Keeper places a
founding group, it grows to what the land holds). It reverses the 2026-11-07 default "no predators". Nothing here is
decided yet.

Markers: **[V]** read in the source, **[S]** secondary or search summary, **[K]** a game or fact as I know it, not
re-checked, **[code]** read in the repo, **[est]** my guess to test. ★ = my recommendation.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- **Wolves fit, and the science gives them a story arc that ends in dogs.** The leading ideas on how dogs began are:
  (1) Ice Age hunters had more lean meat in winter than they could eat, and shared it with wolves (Lahtinen 2021 [S]);
  (2) people took wolf pups and raised them from very young (reviews 2021 and 2023 [S]). Both play out on screen as
  people and wolves living side by side, which is what the user asked for.
- **The rule that keeps pillar 4:** wolves **never hurt a person**. They hunt the animals you placed (with every
  species' floor kept), steal meat from the drying rack, trail the hunters, howl at night and watch from the edge of
  the firelight. Fear is acted out (kids run back, people huddle at the howl), never inflicted. That's also roughly
  real: attacks on people are very rare and mostly come from rabid or food-habituated wolves (NINA 2021 [S]).
- **Placed like the others:** ready once the deer have filled the wood; the Keeper places **a pair at a den** in the
  far wood; pups in spring; **one pack, 2–6 wolves** [est].
- **The arc:** wolves arrive → they hunt deer and you see the chase → the meat thief at the rack (**choice: drive them
  off, or leave them the scraps**) → a bold wolf at the edge of camp → **the lost pup (choice: bring it home?)** → the
  pup grows up with the kids → it joins the hunt and barks when the pack comes near → its pups look **a little more
  like dogs each generation** (floppy ears, a patchy coat, a curled tail: Belyaev's foxes [S]) → in the village the
  dogs guard the duck pen.
- **Five choices** come from wolves (§6). They fit the visit loop: one per visit, and they wait for you.
- **Real behaviour gives us scenes for free:** wolves pick the old and the young (studies of Yellowstone elk [S]),
  ravens come to every kill [S], **deer graze closer to people when wolves are about** (the "human shield": Berger
  2007 [S]), and the hunters can follow the ravens and **chase the wolves off their kill**.
- **Cost:** the wolf, the deer and the dog can share **one four-legged doll**, so wolves add less art than they seem
  to. The new work is the pack's behaviour, the scenes, the choices and the dog line. About 12 new issues, plus edits
  to 4 of the P9 issues already filed (§11).

## 1. What real wolves do (the rules come from here)

| Fact | Source | Game rule |
|---|---|---|
| Wolves select **calves, the old and the weak**. In Yellowstone, 91 % of the adult cow elk wolves killed were 9 or older. Hunters, by contrast, took prime breeding females | Mech et al.; Wright & Lemke 2006; Idaho studies [S] | The pack's chase **tests** the herd and takes an old doe or a fawn. Wolves take less from the deer's growth than the hunters do: the herd holds better than you'd fear |
| Most wolf chases fail; packs run long chases with low success | Oikos 2003 [S] | Most chases end with the deer getting away. A kill is an event, not a routine |
| **Ravens** come to nearly every wolf kill, and remember where kills happen (a 2026 study tracked them flying 150 km+) | *Science* 2026, Yellowstone [S] | Ravens circling over the wood are **the sign of a kill**. The people read it, and so does the player |
| **Howling** spaces packs apart and helps pack mates find each other; replies are likelier at a kill and from bigger packs | Harrington & Mech 1979, 1983 [S] | The howl at dusk is the pack's "we're here". It's louder after a kill. The people go quiet and listen |
| Prey changes **where** it grazes when wolves are around. How much that changes the land is disputed (the Yellowstone "trophic cascade" debate) | NPS; Kauffman et al.; Creel & Christianson 2009 [S] | Deer get **warier**: they graze in shorter bursts and look up more often. No big land effect (the science is shaky, and it would be invisible anyway) |
| Moose mothers moved their calving sites **toward roads**, which bears avoid: prey uses people as a shield | Berger 2007 [S] | **Deer come closer to the camp when the wolves are about.** The hunters find them easier: the wolves help them, which is a lovely surprise |
| Attacks on people are **very rare**. 2002–2020 worldwide: 489, mostly rabid wolves; few predatory ones. Risk factors: **habituation and human food** | NINA report 1944 (Linnell et al. 2021) [S] | **Wolves never touch a person** (pillar 4). Boldness grows with **food left out**, which is the scraps choice (§6.1): bolder means closer, never harm |
| What actually protects herds: **night pens**, **fladry** (a line of flags), **guardian dogs** raised with the herd, and **removing carcasses and bone piles** | Oregon ODFW; USDA; Defenders of Wildlife [S] | The village's answers: a **thorn fence** round the duck pen, **torches** at night, **the dogs** guarding the pen, and burying the bones. Each is something you can see |
| Dogs began from wolves among **hunter-gatherers**. Ideas: shared winter surplus of lean meat (Lahtinen 2021); raising pups taken from dens (2023); the old "camp scavenger" idea is disputed (2021 review) | *Scientific Reports* 2021; *Frontiers* 2021, 2023 [S] | **Both routes are in the game**: scraps make the pack bold, and a pup comes home. They're one story |
| A wolf pup's socialisation window opens at **~2 weeks** (a dog's at ~4). Even with 24-hour contact from before 3 weeks, wolves stay warier than dogs | Lord, UMass [S]; Gácsi 2005; *Sci. Rep.* 2020 [S] | The pup must be **very young** (spring, at the den). The first one stays a bit wild: it won't fetch and it steals food. **Tameness grows over generations**, not in one animal |
| Selecting foxes only for tameness gave **floppy ears, piebald coats, curled tails and puppyish faces** within decades (the "domestication syndrome"; how general this is is debated) | Belyaev / Trut, Novosibirsk, from 1959 [S] | **Each generation of the camp's dogs looks a bit less like a wolf.** Visible change across game years, which the user likes |
| Dogs help hunting **sometimes**, depending on the prey and the method (more deer per hunter-day in Ontario; fewer kills per hour in New Guinea) | Lupo 2017; Godwin 2013; Pattiselanno 2023 [S] | The dog **flushes rabbits** and **tracks a wounded deer** (finds the miss). A small, visible help, not a multiplier |

## 2. How other games do wolves

| Game | Wolves | What we take | What we avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RimWorld** [S] | Pack hunters that will attack and eat pets or colonists when hungry; taming takes handling skill and can fail, turning the wolf **manhunter**; tamed animals **bond** with a colonist | **Bonding**: the pup bonds with one child, who becomes its keeper (a notable thread) | Manhunters, deaths, failed tames that punish |
| **Northgard** [S] | **Dens** keep spawning wolves until cleared; the Fenrir clan summons white wolves and makes wild wolves friendly | Wolves tied to **a place** (the den), and the **white wolf** as something special | Dens as a chore to clear; wolves as an enemy army |
| **Farthest Frontier** [S] | Wolves attack villagers; hunters fight them; dens can be destroyed, but more wolves come | — | All of it. That's the "wolves as a threat" model, which breaks pillar 4 |
| **Minecraft** [S] | Feed a wild wolf bones until it's tame; it gets a **collar**, sits and follows | **One readable sign of tameness**: our first dog gets a hide collar or a bead string. The bone-feeding is our scraps | Instant taming |
| **Valheim** [S] | Taming takes time in a pen: feed it meat **without being seen**, or it loses trust | **Trust grows slowly**, and the people must keep their distance at first | A chore for the player. Here, the people do the taming |
| **Viva Piñata** [S] | Predator species visit and eat others once their needs are met; **sour** pests come to the garden too | Predators as **a rung of the ladder**: they come after their prey is plentiful | Fights and dead piñatas |

**The lesson:** most builders treat wolves as a threat to defend against (Northgard, Farthest Frontier, RimWorld). What
nobody does well is the **relationship**: wolves at the edge of the firelight, slowly becoming the people's dogs. That
gap fits our box (people you watch, pillar 4), and it makes for good scenes.

## 3. Placing the wolves

- **Ready when** the deer have been at capacity for a while [est: ≥ 6 deer for a game year] and the wood is big enough
  for a den away from the camp [est: ≥ 20 grown-tree cells, some ≥ 1.5 box units from the fire]. In the real world
  predators come after their prey, so this rung sits above the deer.
- **Where:** the valid ground is the **far wood**, never near the camp. The den is a hollow under a root or a rock. The
  glow (#423) shows only those spots.
- **The arrival** (#424's pattern): two grey shapes trot out of the trees at the placed spot and sniff the air; the
  first howl that night; the people stop talking and look toward the wood. Glass voice: something like *"Something new
  walks in the dark wood."* (closed-world writing).
- **The pack:** a pair; pups in spring (2–4, playing at the den); young wolves move on gently (they fade out at the
  box's edge in autumn) so the pack stays **2–6** [est]. One pack per box.
- **No re-stock needed:** the pair never leaves (a floor of 2).

## 4. The hard rules (pillar 4, said plainly)

1. **A wolf never touches a person.** Not a child, not a hunter, not while you're away. Tested in the sim and in the
   planner (no plan has contact).
2. **Wolves take animals, never the last of them.** Every species keeps its floor (#422); wolf kills count toward it.
   Wolves only take rabbits, deer and wild ducks; they take from the duck pen only until it's guarded (§6.4).
3. **What wolves steal is small and capped:** a strip of meat from the rack, a hide left out. At most once a season
   [est], never while it would leave the people hungry, and never as a "you lost X" card while you were away.
4. **Fear is acted, not scored.** Kids run back to the fire, someone lifts a torch, everyone goes quiet at the howl.
   Moods may dip a little at night, the way weather does, and that's all.
5. **The people never kill a wolf** ★ (§10, Q1). They drive them off with shouts, torches and fences. So the wolves
   stay, the arc to dogs stays open, and nothing you placed is wiped out.

## 5. What the wolves do: scenes

Research 17's rule: the verb touches its target, and the target answers.

| Scene | Beats | When |
|---|---|---|
| **The pack hunt** | Two or three wolves trot in a line at the wood's edge → the deer lift their heads → the wolves **test** the herd with a short rush; the herd bolts → most often they all get away and the wolves stop, panting → sometimes one old doe lags, the wolves close in, the doe goes down behind a bush (no blood, the bush hides it) → **ravens** circle and land → the wolves eat, then lie down | A few times a game year [est]; more in winter |
| **The howl at dusk** | One wolf sits up and howls, the others join → in the camp everyone falls silent and turns toward the wood → **a child howls back**; a grown-up hushes them; the wolves answer (cute) | Most evenings; louder after a kill |
| **Eyes at the edge** | At night, two pairs of eyes glint at the edge of the firelight → someone throws a stick on the fire, the flames jump → the eyes back off | Some nights; more once the pack is bold |
| **The meat thief** | A wolf slinks toward the drying rack → a hunter sees it and shouts, everyone stands up waving arms and a torch → the wolf grabs a strip and trots off with it, tail up | Once a season at most; it leads to the choice in §6.1 |
| **Trailing the hunters** | When the hunters go after deer, a wolf follows far behind → after the kill, the hunters leave the gut pile; the wolf takes it once they've gone → (later, with scraps) the wolf comes a little closer each time | Each deer hunt, if the pack is bold |
| **The ravens' kill** | Ravens circle over the wood → a hunter points → the hunters walk over, find the wolves at a kill, **shout and wave them off**, and carry the meat home ("power scavenging", real among hunter-gatherers [K]). It's the choice in §6.3 | After a wolf kill |
| **Deer at the camp's edge** | With the wolves about, the deer graze nearer the camp (the human shield); the hunters glance at each other and pick up their bows | While the pack is active |
| **Pups at the den** | Spring: pups tumble and play-fight at the den mouth; an adult lies watching; the kids watch from far off, lying in the grass | Spring |
| **The knock** | Knocking on the glass (exists) startles the wolves, who melt into the trees. The Keeper's only "power" over them, and it's gentle | Any time |

## 6. The choices (visit loop: they wait for you, play live, and "Let them decide" is always there)

### 6.1 The meat thief: drive them off, or leave them the scraps? ★ the hinge
After the first theft: *"The wolves took meat from the rack again. Chase them off, or leave them the scraps?"*
- **Chase them off:** a ring of thorns round the rack and a night torch; the wolves stay wild, keep to the far wood
  and howl from afar. The deer stay warier. **No dogs on this path** (unless you change course, §6.2).
- **Leave the scraps:** a bone spot at the camp's edge. Bit by bit, one bold wolf (the **grey one**, a recognisable
  individual) comes for it at dusk while the people sit and watch. This is the Lahtinen route: winter surplus shared.
  It opens §6.2.
- **Let them decide:** the chief's trait picks (a bold chief shares, a careful one chases).

### 6.2 The lost pup: bring it home? ★ the dog
In spring, on the scraps path (or after 2 springs on the chase path, so it's never closed for good [est]): *"A child
found a wolf pup alone by the den. Bring it home, or leave it with its mother?"*
- **Bring it home:** the child carries it in their arms to the fire. **The dog arc starts (§7).**
- **Leave it:** the pup goes back; the child watches it run to the den. It can come up again next spring.

### 6.3 The wolves' kill: take it, or leave it?
Ravens over the wood: *"The wolves have killed in the wood. Take their meat, or leave it to them?"*
- **Take it:** the hunters drive the wolves off the kill; meat for the camp; the pack is shier for a season.
- **Leave it:** the wolves eat; the pack is a little bolder (closer to the scraps path).

### 6.4 Village: the wolves at the duck pen
The first time the pack circles the pen at night (#431): *"The wolves circled the duck pen last night. A thorn fence,
or let the dogs keep watch?"*
- **A thorn fence:** a ring of thorny branches (real: fladry and night pens); the ducks are safe.
- **The dogs keep watch** (only with dogs): a dog sleeps by the pen; when the wolves come at night it barks and they
  turn back (real: guardian dogs). Seen once, then it's routine.
- Either way, the pen is safe from then on. The choice is about what you see, not about loss.

### 6.5 The white wolf (rare)
Like research 18's white stag: once, a white wolf leads the pack. *"The white wolf howls on the ridge. Let it be, or
will the hunters bring back its pelt?"* ★ Recommend **dropping the pelt option** if wolves are never killed (§4 rule 5).
Then this is a pure moment: everyone comes out to see it, a book page. It isn't a choice at all, which is fine.

## 7. The dog arc: from a pup by the fire to the village's dogs

| Stage | You see | The people | Real anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Scraps** | The grey wolf at the bone spot at dusk; the people sit still and watch | Talk in murmurs; a child edges closer and gets pulled back | Shared winter surplus (Lahtinen 2021) |
| **1 · The pup** | A small grey pup at the fire; it chews a hide, follows the child who found it, sleeps curled by the embers, steals a fish and runs | The child is its **keeper** (bonding, RimWorld's good idea); kids play with it; a grown-up shoos it off the meat | Pups raised from very young (Frontiers 2023) |
| **2 · Grown** | Still wolf-shaped, a hide collar | It **joins the hunts**: flushes rabbits, tracks a wounded deer after a miss (the "miss" from #426 now sometimes ends with the dog finding the deer) | Dogs help in some hunts (Lupo 2017) |
| **2b · The guard** | At night when the pack comes near, it stands up and **barks**; the wild wolves turn back | The meat thief scenes stop; people sleep easy | Guardian dogs |
| **3 · Pups of its own** | Next spring, pups (with a bold wild wolf, or a second adopted pup). **This generation looks a bit different**: one ear flops, the coat has a patch | The camp has 2–3 dogs; kids each have a favourite | Tameness → domestication syndrome (Belyaev) |
| **4 · Generations** | Each new litter drifts: floppier ears, patchier coats, curlier tails, shorter faces. By the village they look like **dogs**, not wolves | Dogs at the tents, at the hunts, at the duck pen (§6.4), lying in the sun | Selection for tameness, in fast-forward |
| **5 · Two worlds** | The wild pack still howls from the far wood; the dogs bark back from the camp | The book has a page: "the first dog" with its keeper's name | — |

Rules [est]: **dogs capped at 1–3** (like the pens: only what's needed). The first dog has a name and a book page.
Dogs never die on screen; an old dog gets grey round the muzzle and lies by the fire more, and in the end it's simply
gone from the next visit, with a history line, the way an old person's death is handled today [check the existing
rule]. **Time is compressed** (domestication took thousands of years); each generation is ~2–3 game years [est].

**Why this is the strongest part:** it's the people's own change, it's visible, and it comes from the Keeper's choice
(the scraps, the pup). It's "care moves up" again: the Keeper placed wild wolves, and the people made friends of them.

## 10. Questions for you (answered 2026-11-07: all ★ defaults, decisions.md)

1. **Do the people ever kill a wolf?** ★ Never: they drive them off · rarely, in a choice (a pelt cloak, the white
   wolf), and the pack keeps its floor.
2. **Is the dog arc closed on the "chase them off" path?** ★ No: the lost pup comes up two springs later anyway, so
   dogs are always possible, only sooner if you share · yes, chasing means no dogs in this box.
3. **How many dogs?** ★ 1–3 · more in a bigger village.
4. **The dogs' look drifting over generations** (floppier ears, patchier coats) ★ yes · no, the first dog already looks
   like a dog.
